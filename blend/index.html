Blend, explained.

Why private leader election needs private broadcast. A 90-second visual explanation of the network, its three roles, and what Core participation means.

Transcript
  1. The source is the secret. A block can be valid — and still reveal its sender. Direct broadcast gives observers an origin to investigate.
  2. Privacy needs both halves. Cryptarchia keeps the leadership election private. Blend helps hide where the winning proposal came from.
  3. Wrap the proposal. The proposer adds layers for selected Blend nodes to decrypt. These layers are not a readable route through the network.
  4. Spread. Don’t draw a route. Encrypted copies spread from peer to peer across the mesh. Only a selected node can remove the next encryption layer.
  5. Break the timing pattern. After decryption, a random wait separates arrival from departure. The final decryptor releases the proposal to ordinary broadcast.
  6. Give messages a crowd. Cover and data messages are designed to be indistinguishable. Proof of Quota limits submissions without identifying the sender.
  7. One network. Three roles. Core nodes mix. Edge nodes inject with less privacy. Too few eligible Core members? Broadcast fallback provides no Blend privacy.
  8. Core is ongoing work. Declare, join epoch membership, relay, and prove activity. Registration is not Core membership. Traffic does not prove accepted activity or rewards.
  9. Public blocks. Obscured origins. Blend makes linking a proposal to its origin harder. The trade-off: extra traffic and delay. Not absolute invisibility.
